MANTRA · TURNING · COMPASSION

Prayer in practice.
From gesture to compassion.

Turning a mani wheel while reciting a mantra belongs to Tibetan Buddhist prayer practice. Shamatha is a meditation that calms and steadies the mind; metta is a distinct practice of loving-kindness, known from the Pali texts. The practices below come from different Buddhist paths, not from stages of one ritual.

MANTRA · MOVEMENT · DEDICATION

The mantra sounds.
The hand turns.
The goodness is dedicated to all.

The meaning of a prayer gesture lies not in the number of turns. It unfolds through recitation, intention, and the way a wish for well-being becomes action.

✳

DISTINCT PATHS · A SHARED ASPIRATION

Recitation. Circumambulation.
Loving-kindness.

These practices do not form a single prescribed sequence. Choose one for today; you may close with a brief dedication of goodness to all living beings.

01

Turning a mani wheel

Hold the handheld wheel gently and let the cylinder turn evenly. Accompany the movement by reciting “Om mani padme hum” or keeping a wish of compassion in mind. There is no need to hurry; the rhythm of prayer matters more than speed.

Tibetan Buddhism · prayerful gesture
02

Recitation with a mālā

Recite the six-syllable mantra gently and clearly. If you use a mālā, move one bead with each repetition. Let counting help you keep your place, not turn prayer into a race.

Mantra · sound and aspiration
03

Circumambulating a stupa

Pilgrims circumambulate stupas as part of prayer. Walk calmly, reciting the mantra or wishing well to all beings; let each step express reverence and compassion.

Pilgrimage · kora
04

Shamatha: settling the mind

Choose the natural breath or the sound of the mantra as an anchor. When thoughts distract you, do not scold yourself; gently return to the breath or the next syllable. In this way, the mind learns to settle without force.

Meditation · collectedness
05

Metta: loving-kindness for all

In metta practice, the wish of loving-kindness is extended to all beings—near and far, seen and unseen. Repeat: “May all beings be safe, free from hostility, and find peace.” You do not need to force a strong feeling; a sincere wish is enough.

Metta-bhāvanā · loving-kindness
06

Dedication of merit

At the end, dedicate inwardly to all living beings the goodness that arose through recitation and turning. In Mahayana Buddhism, this gesture connects personal effort with the aspiration to awaken for the benefit of others.

Mahayana · dedication of goodness

CALM · CLEAR SEEING · KINDNESS

Steady the mind.
See more clearly.

In many Buddhist teachings, shamatha calms and steadies the mind, while vipassana explores the changing nature of experience. Metta cultivates loving-kindness. These are distinct ways to deepen one’s practice and care for living beings.

01

Shamatha · concentration

Sit steadily and choose the breath or the sound of the mantra as your anchor. When the mind wanders, return to it calmly. The aim is not to stop thoughts, but to avoid being carried away by each one.

02

Vipassana · insight

Notice how sensations, feelings, and thoughts arise, change, and pass away. Neither suppress nor cling to them; observe experience as it unfolds from moment to moment.

03

Metta-bhāvanā · loving-kindness

May the heart open to the wish for happiness for all beings—strong and vulnerable, near and far, seen and unseen.

The Avalokiteshvara mantra and the Pali practice of metta are presented here in their respective contexts. They are distinct practices, not parts of one prescribed ritual. If breath-focused practice causes tension, open your eyes and choose the sound of the mantra or the feeling of your feet on the ground.

FIVE MINUTES · MANTRA AND DEDICATION

A quiet recitation.
A gentle close.

Choose a quiet place and settle comfortably. Recite the mantra at a natural pace, then dedicate the practice to the well-being of all beings. Five minutes is enough for a short, complete prayer practice.

◌

If attention to the breath feels uncomfortable, do not try to change your breathing. Open your eyes and feel the support beneath your feet. You can continue with the mantra or end the practice.

01 · 1 MIN

Settle comfortably

Sit comfortably, lengthen your spine without strain, and rest your hands on your knees. For a moment, remember to whom you wish to dedicate the prayer.

02 · 3 MIN

Recite the mantra

Repeat “Om mani padme hum” aloud, in a whisper, or silently. When a thought carries you away, gently return to the next syllable; let the words flow evenly and freely.

03 · 1 MIN

Dedicate the practice

Pause briefly and wish: “May all beings be free from suffering and find peace.” Dedicate whatever goodness has arisen to the well-being of all living beings.

May each step and word
lead toward compassion.
