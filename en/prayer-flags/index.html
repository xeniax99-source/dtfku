LUNGTA · WIND · GOOD WISHES

Prayer flags: colour meanings and the lungta tradition

Prayer flags are especially associated with Tibetan and Himalayan cultures. Mantras, prayers and symbols are printed on the cloth; lungta means “wind horse” and is often associated with horizontal strings of flags.

LUNGTA · “WIND HORSE”

Prayer texts in the open air

Flags may show the wind horse, auspicious symbols, mantras and prayers. In traditional understanding, the wind moving the cloth serves as an image of good wishes spreading. This is a religious and poetic meaning, not a literal promise that flags change events.

A common lungta garland uses square or rectangular cloth panels attached along their top edge to a cord and hung so the fabric can move in the wind. They are not triangular pennants. Vertical banners on poles are also used; local names and customs vary.

FIVE COLOURS

A common interpretation, not the only scheme

One widespread explanation connects the five colors with natural elements. The cards show common associations, not a universal chart; explanations vary by school and region.

  • BlueSpace or sky
  • WhiteAir or wind
  • RedFire
  • GreenWater
  • YellowEarth

A RESPECTFUL APPROACH

Context, place and care for the cloth

Communities have customs about where and how to hang flags, when to replace worn cloth and how to treat prayer texts. Follow local guidance where available. Outside a religious setting, avoid reducing the flags to decoration alone; for many people they are objects of prayer.
