AVALOKITESHVARA MANTRA · RECITATION · DEDICATION

Om mani padme hum.
Six syllables of compassion.

“Om mani padme hum” is associated with Avalokiteshvara, the bodhisattva of compassion. In Tibetan Buddhism, practitioners recite it in prayer and inscribe it on the scrolls placed inside mani wheels. Recitation joins the sound of the mantra with the breath and an intention to keep compassion present; the practice may be dedicated to the well-being of all beings.

INTENTION · SOUND · DEDICATION

Syllable by syllable.
Turn by turn.
Compassion in action.

The mantra is not recited as a promise of a particular result. Its sound recalls compassion and can help direct the prayer beyond oneself, toward all living beings.

ॐ

THREE MOVEMENTS OF PRAYER

Begin with trust.
Conclude with dedication.

Many Mahayana prayers hold three movements: taking refuge, aspiring to awakening, and dedicating merit. These are not required words to recite, but a direction for the heart: acknowledge the path, wish others well, and dedicate the fruits of wholesome action to all beings.

01 / TAKING REFUGE

Taking refuge

The Buddha is the awakened one, the Dharma his teaching and path, and the Sangha the community of practitioners. Taking refuge expresses trust in these Three Jewels and a resolve to follow the path to awakening.

02 / ASPIRATION

Aspiration to awakening

Set an intention: may this practice strengthen wisdom and compassion, and your capacity to respond to the suffering of others.

03 / DEDICATION

Dedication of merit

As you close the prayer, wish that whatever goodness arose through the practice may serve the awakening and well-being of all living beings.

May all living beings be free from suffering. May they know peace, safety, and the causes of happiness.

A metta wish: loving-kindness extended to all beings.

AVALOKITESHVARA · BODHISATTVA OF COMPASSION

Sanskrit mantra · six syllables

Om mani padme hum

Avalokiteshvara is the bodhisattva of compassion, known in Tibet as Chenrezig. The mantra is recited in temples, while walking, and while turning a mani wheel. Repeating it recalls the aspiration to help living beings and bring compassion into everyday actions.

In one Tibetan interpretation, “mani”, the jewel, symbolizes method and compassion; “padme”, the lotus, symbolizes wisdom; and “hum” points to their inseparability. This is a symbolic commentary on the mantra, not a literal translation of each syllable.

SANSKRIT TRANSLITERATION

Oṃ maṇi padme hūṃ

This is one traditional symbolic interpretation of the mantra.

A SHORT PRAYER PRACTICE

Settle. Recite.
Dedicate.

Settle comfortably. If you use a mālā, hold it gently. Recite the mantra steadily, without rushing or trying to produce a particular state. At the end, pause for a moment and dedicate the practice to the well-being of all beings.

01 / INTENTION

Set an intention

Sit steadily, soften your shoulders, and remember why you wish to recite the mantra. You may dedicate these minutes to the well-being of someone close to you or of all living beings.

02 / RECITATION

Recite the mantra

Recite “Om mani padme hum” aloud, in a whisper, or silently. Listen to the whole mantra; if you use a mālā, move one bead with each repetition. Do not force the syllables to match the turning of the wheel.

03 / DEDICATION

Dedicate the fruits

Rest in silence for a few moments. Then wish that the goodness arising from the prayer may become a cause for freedom from suffering and happiness for all living beings.

HEART SUTRA · MAHAYANA

The closing mantra
of the Heart Sutra.

In many versions of the Heart Sutra, recitation concludes with the Sanskrit mantra “Gate gate pāragate pārasaṃgate bodhi svāhā”. It is often kept in its original sound, bringing the teaching to a close with a movement beyond delusion toward awakening.

“Gate gate pāragate pārasaṃgate bodhi svāhā”

The Sanskrit formula in transliteration

This line is not well served by a mechanical, word-by-word translation. Traditional readings hear in the repeated “gate” a movement toward and beyond awakening; the meaning of the whole sutra matters more than its final formula alone.

May prayer lead to kind action.
