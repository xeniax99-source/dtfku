MĀLĀ · COUNTING MANTRAS · DIFFERENT INTERPRETATIONS

Why is the number 108 considered special in Buddhism?

The number 108 appears in many Asian religious cultures and in Buddhist practices, but no single explanation is shared by every school. Its role may be symbolic, or it may simply mark a traditional cycle for counting.

MĀLĀ AND RECITATION

Beads help keep count

A mālā is a string of beads used to count mantras, prayers or other repetitions. Many Buddhist traditions use a 108-bead mālā; shorter forms such as 54 or 27 beads are also found. The count can structure a session, but it does not measure how deeply someone is focused.

WHY 108?

Several explanations, not one universal formula

Some Buddhist explanations associate 108 with mental afflictions or experiences that practitioners seek to recognize and overcome. In other contexts, it marks a traditional full cycle of recitation. Schools and sources offer different lists and symbolic readings; they should not be reduced to a single mathematical explanation.

108 IN PRACTICE

A useful count, not a substitute for understanding

Reciting a mantra 108 times is a common form of practice, not a universal rule. A teacher or lineage may use a different count, structure or emphasis on the quality of attention. Understanding the purpose of a particular practice matters more than simply reaching a target number.
